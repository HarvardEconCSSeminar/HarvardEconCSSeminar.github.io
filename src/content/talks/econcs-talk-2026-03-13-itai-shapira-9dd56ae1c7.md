Large language models are often sycophantic, meaning that they affirm a user’s stated or implied belief even when doing so conflicts with factual accuracy or sound judgment. This presentation examines that failure mode through the incentives induced by preference-based post-training. We provide a formal analysis of how bias in human preference comparisons can lead the learned reward to assign higher value to stance-affirming responses than to corrective ones. Using standard random utility models such as Bradley–Terry, we characterize when this bias arises in reward learning and show how optimization against the resulting reward can amplify agreement with false premises. The analysis identifies a mechanism by which sycophancy emerges in the standard reward-learning and post-training pipeline.
