The rise of algorithmic pricing raises concerns that algorithms might collude to maximize firm profits, to the detriment of consumers. We conduct experiments with algorithmic pricing agents based on Large Language Models (LLMs), and specifically on OpenAI's GPT-4. We find that (1) LLM-based pricing agents are adept at pricing tasks, (2) LLM-based pricing agents autonomously collude in oligopoly settings, and (3) variation in seemingly innocuous phrases in LLM instructions ("prompts") may lead to substantially higher levels of collusion. Our findings underscore the need for antitrust regulation regarding algorithmic pricing, and uncover novel regulatory challenges unique to LLM-based pricing agents.
