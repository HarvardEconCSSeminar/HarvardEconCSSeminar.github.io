I will present work that initiates the study of consumer utility maximization in Bayesian multidimensional settings and online single-dimensional settings, both well-studied for the objectives of revenue and welfare. However, when non-monetary payments are used to incentivize agents, these "payments" come in the form of "ordeals" like bureaucratic paperwork or waiting in long lines, and cannot be transferred to the seller, thus they do not benefit anyone. This changes the mechanism designer's objective, and requires trading off efficiency in the allocation with the minimization of these ordeals. First, I will discuss simple, approximately-optimal mechanisms for the i.i.d. unit-demand (matching) setting, targeting the simplest benchmark of welfare. Then, I will focus on online prior-free mechanism design in the single item setting (the secretary setting), and augmenting the mechanism with machine-learned predictions. I will highlight how utility maximization results in fundamentally different analyses and mechanisms than welfare- and revenue-maximization, and other insights learned. Joint work with Taylor Lundy, Divyarthi Mohan, and Thodoris Tsilivis.
