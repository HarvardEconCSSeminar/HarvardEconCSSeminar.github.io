Welfare maximization in bilateral trade is a central objective in mechanism design, but most incentive-compatible approximation guarantees rely on the private-values assumption. This talk studies welfare maximization in bilateral trade with interdependent values, where each agent’s value may depend on the other’s private information, making mechanism design substantially more challenging due to belief updates and strategic inference. We propose a way to classify information structures by quantifying how strongly an agent’s private signal influences their own valuation, and use this framework to map out when approximation is possible versus impossible. We also characterize achievable approximation ratios for a natural family of information structures. Based on Interdependent Bilateral Trade: Information vs Approximation (EC 2025).
