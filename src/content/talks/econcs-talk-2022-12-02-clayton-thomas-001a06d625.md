A menu description defines a mechanism to player i in two steps. Step (1) uses the reports of other players to describe i’s menu: the set of i’s potential outcomes. Step (2) uses i’s report to select i’s favorite outcome from her menu. Can menu descriptions better expose strategyproofness, without sacrificing simplicity? We propose a new, simple menu description of Deferred Acceptance, the most popular strategyproof matching mechanism. We prove that—in contrast with other common matching mechanisms—this menu description must differ substantially from the corresponding traditional description, and that descriptions face a large formal tradeoff between conveying strategyproofness to player i and conveying the rest of the matching. We demonstrate, with a lab experiment on two simple mechanisms, the promise and challenges of menu descriptions.
